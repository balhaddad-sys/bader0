The brainstem and cord in five slices

Each brainstem level carries its own cranial nerve nuclei beside the same long tracts; in the cord, the corticospinal tract and dorsal columns have not yet crossed. Tap a structure to see what it does and what a lesion there looks like.

The rule of 4

A useful scaffold for brainstem anatomy, with exceptions. Motor nuclei of III, IV, VI and XII lie near the midline; the other cranial motor nuclei do not follow that rule.

Ten rules to take to the bedside

How the ranking works

This is an unvalidated, transparent educational model. Weights describe typical anatomical associations: 3 defining, 2 typical, 1 supportive. An “excluding” finding is a strong mismatch in this model, not an absolute clinical exclusion. Priors break exact ties only.

Fit = E / (E + A + U + C + O)

  • E, evidence: the weights of present findings, plus 0.5 for each excluder explicitly marked tested normal.
  • A, absent expected signs: full weight for expected signs weighted 2 or 3; half weight for signs weighted 1.
  • U, untested signs: 0.25 × weight for each untested finding weighted 2 or 3.
  • C, contradictions: 3 for each excluder marked present.
  • O, unexplained findings: 1.5 for each present finding not accounted for; 0.5 if it is soft.

A site is shown only when at least one present finding has weight 2 or 3. Results sort by fit, then evidence, then prior. Untested and tested normal are deliberately different.

Sides. Each sided finding is recorded on the right, left or both sides. Every expected sign is defined relative to the lesion: on the same side (a facial palsy in the pons), the opposite side (weakness from a pontine lesion) or both. A one-sided site is scored with the lesion on the right and on the left, and the better fit gives the lesion side. A sign on the unexpected side is not counted as evidence; a sign on both sides earns half the weight of a one-sided expectation. Language areas are taken to lie in the left hemisphere, as in nearly all right-handed people.

“Examine next” compares the top four eligible sites before any map filter. Each relevant untested sign is simulated as present and tested normal. For each outcome we measure the range of fit changes across those sites; the larger range is its discrimination score. Up to five signs exceeding 0.08 are shown. Tapping a suggestion opens its bedside description and lets you record present or tested normal. Leaving it untested does not change the examination.

Map staining uses (the region’s best fit ÷ the overall best fit)4. The number in each region is its best actual fit. The outline marks the leading region. Side labels relate to the selected side of cranial signs; visual field labels explicitly name the side of field loss. Bilateral and diffuse patterns cannot be assigned a single side.

Sections shade a structure on the side implied by each present finding (crossed tracts on the other side from the deficit in the brainstem, the same side in the cord) and outline it as spared when its findings were tested normal.

Practice cases take every defining sign of one site, most of its typical signs and a few supportive ones, plus two or three of its excluding signs marked normal. A case is used only when that site ranks first by at least 0.04; the other choices are its nearest rivals in the model and one wider distractor.
