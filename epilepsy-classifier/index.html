Physical signs

Examination findings that point toward a syndrome or a cause: facial and head features, neurocutaneous skin signs, tone, strength and vision, and movement and coordination. Each entry shows the sign, which ILAE criteria use it, what it can be mistaken for, and where the wording comes from (ILAE papers, international consensus criteria, or standard clinical teaching). Drawings are schematic and skin signs look different across skin tones and ages.

Body proportions by age

The drawings can show an infant, child or adult (Movements tab). Standard anatomy: the head is about a quarter of body length at birth, about a fifth at 2 years, and about an eighth in adults — so infants have large heads, short limbs and a short trunk, and falls and postures look different.

Seizure movements & poses

What the body does during a seizure, drawn frame by frame: tonic and fencing postures, the figure-of-4 sign, version, clonic jerking, spasms, drops, hyperkinetic movements, automatisms and the face and eyes. The patient's right is on the viewer's left; orange limbs are the moving or posturing parts. Drawings are stylised teaching schematics, not clinical photographs, and each entry states whether its wording comes from the ILAE papers or from standard semiology teaching.
